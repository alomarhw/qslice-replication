\title{Static Program Slicing for Quantum Programs}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Author(s)}}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Quantum programs are increasingly written in general-purpose languages, most often in Python with Qiskit, where loops,
functions and computed indices decide which gates act on which qubits. While classical programs are supported by
mature slicing tools, quantum programs have almost none, and developers still debug them by printing, drawing
circuits and reading code. This work brings static program slicing to quantum programs. The srcML infrastructure and
the srcSlice slicer are extended with a quantum layer that identifies the quantum role of each call, traces the circuit
a program builds without executing it, and slices every qubit along the gates and measured bits that can affect it,
including loops that run on the device. The approach is implemented in a tool, \tool{}, which reports a slice as source
lines, the loop iterations that put them in it, and the classical statements that compute them. The slices are
accurate. On \numToolDevPrograms{} programs the static trace equals the circuit Qiskit builds and the slices equal those
computed on that circuit in \numToolDevSliceEqual{}, and on \numQasmCircuitsChecked{} benchmark circuits the OpenQASM
reader agrees with Qiskit. Exact simulation confirms every qubit's slice in programs with loops on the device, and the
classical statements cover those a reference computation finds for \numSsContainPct{} of qubits. Of
\numScaleSOneFiles{} Qiskit programs from GitHub, \tool{} traces a circuit in \numScaleSOneSliced{}, and
\numCovNoConstruction{} of the others never build one. It traces \numQheTracedOfBuildsPct{} of the expert-written
Qiskit HumanEval programs that build a circuit. It slices a program in \numScaleSOneMedianMs{}~ms in the median and
every qubit of a circuit of \numScaleQbMaxOps{} operations in \numScaleQbAllS{}~s after \numScaleQbReadS{}~s of
reading, and a qubit's slice holds \numScaleReadMedianPct{} of a program's code lines in the median.
\end{abstract}
