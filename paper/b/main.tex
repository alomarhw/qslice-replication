\title{Static Program Slicing for Quantum Programs}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Author(s)}}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Which statements of a quantum program determine the state of a qubit? In Qiskit programs this is hard to see, because
loops, functions and computed indices decide which gates act on which qubits, and a gate can change a qubit it does not
target. Slicing answers such questions for classical programs, but quantum programs have almost no slicing support.
This work extends the srcML infrastructure and the srcSlice slicer with a quantum layer. The layer identifies the
quantum role of each call, traces the circuit a program builds without executing it, and slices every qubit along the
gates and measured bits that can affect it, including loops that run on the device. The resulting tool, \tool{}, shows
a slice as source lines, the loop iterations that put them there and the classical statements that compute them. On
\numToolDevPrograms{} programs its traces match the circuits Qiskit builds and its slices match circuit-level slices
in all but one, and exact simulation confirms its slices of programs with device loops. Its classical statements
include all those a reference computation finds for \numSsContainPct{} of qubits, and for \numSsHeldContainPct{} in
held-out programs. \tool{} traces a circuit in \numScaleSOneSliced{} of \numScaleSOneFiles{} GitHub programs, and most
of the others never build one. It slices a program in \numScaleSOneMedianMs{}~ms in the median, and a qubit's slice
holds \numScaleReadMedianPct{} of the program's code lines.
\end{abstract}
