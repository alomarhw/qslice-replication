\title{What Must a Quantum Slice Keep? Slicing Quantum Programs for Comprehension}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Author(s)}}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Which statements must a slice of a quantum program keep to preserve the state of selected qubits? The answer is
fundamental to using slicing for program comprehension, yet the dependence rules proposed for quantum programs have not
been evaluated against such a criterion. We define a correct slice as one that preserves the selected qubits' joint
state for every input state, and an operation as a dependence when a change right after it can alter that state. Our approach builds the quantum dependence graph (QDG) from a write-all rule, under which a gate writes every
qubit it acts on, its controls included, and we prove that QDG slices preserve the required state and retain every
dependence. Classical
data, classical control, and quantum data dependence suffice, and entanglement is neither necessary nor sufficient for
dependence. We compare the QDG with alternative dependence rules on \numNPairs{}
circuit--qubit pairs and \numStudyFaultUnits{} fault units. Treating control qubits as read-only, as the classical
program dependence graph does, produces incorrect slices for \numCpdgUnsoundPct{} of the pairs. Of these,
\numFdCpdgZMatchPct{} still give the qubit the same frequencies of measured outcomes as the whole program, so the
histogram a developer would check hides the error. The previously proposed quantum program dependence graph
(QPDG), which adds entanglement edges, retains \numYzLcExcessRatio{} times as many operations that cannot affect the selected qubits as the
QDG, and pruning slices to make them smaller removes genuine dependences. QDG slices contain \numRqThreeContainLc{} of
the seeded faults and take milliseconds to compute, although they do not improve the ranking of mutation-based fault
localization. These findings provide a semantic foundation for quantum slicing and clarify its benefits and limits for
comprehension and debugging.
\end{abstract}
