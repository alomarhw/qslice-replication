\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

Before Section~\ref{sec:core} defines the semantics, this section reviews four lines of research and the gaps they
leave open.

\subsection{Program slicing and its semantics}
Weiser introduced program slicing~\cite{weiser1984program} and showed that programmers use slices when they
debug~\cite{weiser1982programmers}, and slicing is a comprehension aid as much as a debugging
one~\cite{gallagher1991using}. Dependence graphs reduced slicing to reachability~\cite{ottenstein1984pdg,
ferrante1987pdg, horwitz1990interprocedural}, Podgurski and Clarke defined dependence
semantically~\cite{podgurski1990formal}, and dynamic slicing restricts a slice to one execution~\cite{korel1988dynamic,
agrawal1990dynamic}. A backward slice gives the statements that can affect a value at a point, and its dual, the
forward slice, the statements a given statement can affect~\cite{tip1994a, horwitz1990interprocedural}. Surveys catalog the kinds of slices that followed~\cite{tip1994a, silva2012vocabulary, binkley2004a}.
Probabilistic slicing preserves the distribution of the criterion variables~\cite{hur2014slicing, amtoft2020theory,
olmedo2025slicing}, and Gallagher and Kozaitis name quantum programs as a direction for
slicing~\cite{gallagher2025program}. For quantum programs, the open question is what such a slice must preserve.

\subsection{Dependence models for quantum programs}
Yu and Zhao's quantum program dependence graph (QPDG)~\cite{yu2025qpdg} extends the classical
PDG~\cite{ferrante1987pdg} with entanglement, no-cloning, temporal and measurement dependences and proposes slicing on
it. It is defined in prose with examples and has been neither implemented nor evaluated, and in the graph drawn for
its first listing the slice of a measured qubit omits the two gates that fix its outcome, so the slice returns 1 with
probability \numYzFigSliceProb{} where the program returns 1 with certainty. Kaul et al.'s quantum code property graph
(QCPG) for Qiskit and OpenQASM is queried for bugs and smells, among them gates that do not affect a measured
qubit~\cite{kaul2023uniform}. Its data-flow edges run from a CNOT's control to its target only, since the authors
state that the control is unchanged, so the query can wrongly report as superfluous a gate whose effect reaches a measured qubit through
a control. The graph is demonstrated on example programs.

Behler et al.\ derive the order of qubit operations from
OpenQASM~3 source to detect patterns and suggest refactorings, and their order table marks a CNOT's control apart
from its target~\cite{behler2024supporting, behler2025static}. Murillo et al.\ describe the QPDG as an aid to
understanding programs~\cite{murillo2025roadmap}, and Ramalho et al.\ find dependence analysis of circuits almost
unexplored~\cite{ramalho2025road}. The QPDG's entanglement edges rest on analyses that approximate which qubits may be correlated, with type
systems~\cite{perdrix2007types, prost2009reasoning, yuan2022twist}, abstract interpretation~\cite{perdrix2008entanglement,
yu2021abstract}, the stabilizer formalism~\cite{honda2015stabilizer} and data-flow rules~\cite{xia2025entanglement},
and ScaffCC assumes that interacting qubits may be entangled~\cite{javadiabhari2015scaffcc}. None of them slices.

\subsection{Light cones and compilers}
The \emph{backward light cone} of a set of qubits at the end of a circuit is the set of gates that can affect their
final state. Compilers schedule and map circuits on gate-dependence graphs built on it~\cite{murali2019triq,
amy2020staq}, analyses of the quantum approximate optimization algorithm (QAOA) use it to bound the gates that can
reach an observable~\cite{farhi2014qaoa}, and quantum
separation logics reason locally about the qubits a program touches~\cite{zhou2021qsl, le2022qsl}. Qiskit's
\texttt{LightCone} pass~\cite{qiskit2025} walks a circuit backward from given qubits or an observable on them, a quantity
measured on the qubits such as a Pauli operator, keeps the gates in the light cone and also removes each gate that
commutes with the observable, since such a gate cannot change its expected measured value. It decides commutation for the circuit's actual angles and refuses an observable on a circuit
that contains measurements. Heim et al.\ likewise argue that quantum dependences should account for
commutation~\cite{heim2020quantum}.

\subsection{Debugging tools and bug detectors}
Metwalli and Van Meter split a circuit at barriers and remove the qubits a part does not
use~\cite{metwalli2022debugging, metwalli2024testing}, and Qiskit's circuit-slicing utilities cut a circuit by
barriers, depth or gate type~\cite{qiskitaddonutils}. Neither follows dependences. Bug detectors find known
patterns~\cite{chen2023smelly, zhao2023qchecker, nayak2023qpac}. LintQ orders two operations that share a
qubit~\cite{paltenghi2024lintq}, which is the relation of the write-all rule, but uses it for patterns, not slices. Of
the 42 Bugs4Q bugs on which QChecker was evaluated, 22 are wrong outputs that no pattern
detects~\cite{zhao2023qchecker}, and a slice shows where such a fault can be. We reuse Bugs4Q~\cite{zhao2023bugs4q},
mutation tools~\cite{mendiluze2021muskit, fortunato2022qmutpy, garcia2022testing} and mutation-based fault
localization~\cite{ishimoto2025mbfl}.

\subsection{Open gaps and the data used}
Table~\ref{tab:related} compares the dependence models these works use and shows that none of them both defines
what a slice must preserve and guarantees it. Three gaps remain. First, no rule proposed
for quantum slicing has been checked against what a quantum program computes, and the one slice worked out in detail,
in the QPDG's first example, is wrong. Second, the QCPG reads a gate's control as unchanged and the order tables of
Behler et al.\ record the control only as a separate role, and no work has measured what reading a control as
unchanged does to a slice. Third, the light cone is known to be sound, but no work
has asked whether it keeps every dependence, how far it is from the smallest slice that does, and what smaller rules
lose. We keep the classical notions of a criterion, a backward and a forward slice and dependence as the possibility
of change, and define them for the joint state of a set of qubits, which unlike a distribution in one basis keeps
phase. Section~\ref{sec:kinds} shows that entanglement neither implies nor is implied by dependence, so entanglement
analyses cannot decide what a slice keeps. The dependence graphs were shown on examples and the detectors evaluated on
Bugs4Q and on LintQ's corpus, so we use Bugs4Q for real faults and add circuits with a simulated ground truth and
seeded faults.
