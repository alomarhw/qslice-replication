One choice decides soundness, what a controlled gate writes. The classical PDG misses \numCpdgMissTotal{}
dependences over the \numNPairs{} pairs, and all but \numCpdgMissFeedback{}, \numCpdgMissControlPct{}, follow from
reading a control as unchanged. Of the misses, \numCpdgMissSameWire{} are controlled gates whose control is the
criterion qubit, \numCpdgMissKickback{} reach the criterion only through such a gate, \numCpdgMissWritten{} through a
control further along the chain, and \numCpdgMissFeedback{} through classical feedback. Table~\ref{tab:rq1} shows
that the classical PDG gives a wrong slice for \numCpdgUnsound{} pairs, \numCpdgUnsoundPct{} (95\% interval
\numCcCpdgUnsound{} with the \numCcFamilies{} algorithm families resampled as clusters). Its control dependence
matters only on dynamic circuits, where it is wrong on \numCpdgDynamicUnsound{} pair against \numDuDynamicUnsound{}
for def-use.

Many of these wrong slices look right. Of the \numCpdgUnsound{}, \numFdCpdgZMatch{} (\numFdCpdgZMatchPct{}) give the
criterion the whole circuit's outcome distribution in the computational basis, so only \numFdCpdgObservable{} pairs,
\numFdCpdgObservablePct{} of all, are wrong in what a terminal measurement reads. The others are wrong in phase, which
a histogram check accepts but which changes the outcome once the slice is composed with later gates or measured in
another basis. Testing meets the same limit with phase-flip faults~\cite{oldfield2024faster}. As H1 holds by
construction, state-based pruning, which
starts from the QDG slice, is wrong on fewer pairs than def-use, \numRqOneQUnsound{} against \numRqOneDuUnsound{}
(exact McNemar, Holm \numHOnePHolm{}).

The QDG preserves the criterion on every pair at every input checked and recovers every measured dependence, as Theorem~\ref{thm:sound} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:complete} predict, and Table~\ref{tab:rq1} gives each rule's recall and precision against the
dependence set. The QPDG is sound too, since its slices contain the QDG's, but its
entanglement edges add only operations that are not dependences, and its slices grow from \numRqOneLcSize{} to
\numRqOneYzSize{} of the circuit. The flow-sensitive variant still keeps \numYzfExcess{} points above the dependence
set, and a graph with one entanglement edge per two-qubit gate gives exactly the QDG slice on all
\numFdFourEdgeNoClosureEqual{} pairs it applies to, so the excess comes from closing these edges transitively. Under the
reading in which a controlled gate writes only its targets, the QPDG is wrong on \numYzRoUnsound{} pairs. On the
\numCorpusLSoundPairs{} pairs of corpus~L that the reference simulator can check exactly, the QDG and the QPDG are sound everywhere,
while def-use is wrong on \numCorpusLWrongDu{}.

The rules nest. On all \numNestPairs{} pairs the dependence set lies within the QDG slice and the QDG slice within
the QPDG slice, and Table~\ref{tab:sizerob} shows how far apart they are. Restricted to its dependence set, every circuit
preserves the criterion at $\ket{0\dots0}$, so at that input the dependence set is itself a correct slice, the
smallest one that contains every dependence. It makes up \numDepFloor{} of a circuit on average. The QDG slice keeps only
\numLcExcess{} percentage points more and equals it on \numLcExact{} of the \numNPairs{} pairs, while the QPDG keeps
\numYzExcess{} points more, \numYzLcExcessRatio{} times as many operations that cannot affect the
criterion. The gap grows
with width. At \numWElevenLo{} to \numWElevenHi{} qubits the QDG slice keeps \numWElevenLc{} of the circuit and on
corpus~L \numCorpusLSizeLc{}, while the QPDG stays near \numCorpusLSizeYz{}, because it links the operations of a whole
coupling component. On the pairs where it applies, commutation pruning falls below the dependence set, to \numWElevenLcq{} at the same widths,
because it is sound for fixed angles but not complete, and can drop an operation where a fault would change the
criterion, like the identity gate of line~38 in Fig.~\ref{fig:bv}.

Forward slices behave alike. For all \numFdFwdOps{} operations of corpus~A, the forward slice contains every measured
forward dependence and agrees with the backward slices, at a precision of \numFdFwdPrecision{}.

Language models miss dependences that the rule finds. Asked to list the operations that can affect a qubit, Claude
Sonnet~5.5 and Haiku~5.5 recall \numTOneSonnetRecall{} and \numTOneHaikuRecall{} of the dependences and both Qwen
models \numTOneQwenMidRecall{}, and Sonnet recalls only \numTOneSonnetKick{} of those through kickback.

\emph{Answer to RQ1.} Only rules that let a gate write all its qubits are sound. Def-use and the classical PDG give
wrong slices for \numRqOneDuUnsound{} and \numCpdgUnsound{} of the \numNPairs{} pairs, and pruning is correct at
$\ket{0\dots0}$ but drops dependences. The QDG and the QPDG are sound and dependence-complete, and the QDG adds the
least code beyond the dependences.

\subsection{RQ2: How much smaller than the QDG slice can a slice safely be, and under which inputs?}

Both refinements make slices smaller, but only commutation pruning keeps them correct for fixed angles. State-based
pruning shrinks the QDG slice by \numHTwoDiff{} percentage points of the circuit (95\% CI \numHTwoCILow{} to
\numHTwoCIHigh{}) and is smaller on \numHTwoSmaller{} pairs (H2, Wilcoxon, Holm \numHTwoPHolm{}).
Table~\ref{tab:sizerob} shows the cost. Of the pairs it prunes, the slice stays correct for all random product inputs
on only \numHThreePrunedRate{}, and over all pairs on \numHThreeRate{} (Wilson CI \numHThreeCILow{} to
\numHThreeCIHigh{}, \numCcHThree{} with families as clusters, Holm \numHThreePHolm{}), far below the threshold of
95\%, so H3 fails.

Commutation pruning is smaller than the QDG slice on \numLcqSmallerThanLc{} of \numLcqApplicable{} pairs, stays
correct on every random basis and product input, and fails only when the angles change, on \numRobLcqParamFail{} of
\numRobLcqParamN{} pairs of variational circuits. Its symbolic variant, at a mean size of \numLcqsSize{} against
\numLcqSize{}, stays correct everywhere, but recalls of \numLcqRecall{} and \numLcqsRecall{} show that both variants
miss dependences.

Safety also means behaving like the program on a noisy device. Under depolarizing noise, the criterion's states under the QDG slice and the whole circuit differ by a trace distance
of at most \numNoiseLcCrossExactMax{}, which is rounding error, as Theorem~\ref{thm:sound} predicts for noise that
acts on a gate's own qubits, while the state-based slice differs in \numNoiseQCrossExactNonzero{} of \numNoiseQCrossExactN{} exact runs, by up to
\numNoiseQCrossExactMax{}, because it removes noisy gates that the program executes~\cite{preskill2018nisq}.

\emph{Answer to RQ2.} Commutation pruning is the only refinement that stays correct under other inputs, for the
circuit's angles, and it is smaller than the QDG slice on \numLcqSmallerThanLc{} of \numLcqApplicable{} pairs. A
state-based slice holds for its own input only. Neither keeps every dependence.

Table~\ref{tab:rq3} shows that a slice that contains the fault more often is also larger, so containment has to be
read against a random set of the slice's size. The QDG slice contains the fault in \numRqThreeContainLc{} of the units
and the QPDG slice in \numRqThreeContainYz{}, but a random set of the QPDG's size already contains it in
\numSizeCtlYzRandom{}. Every rule beats a random set of its own size, so containment is not only a matter of size.
The QDG gains \numSizeCtlLcGain{} points over random (family CI \numSizeCtlLcGainCi{}), the smaller slices
\numSizeCtlCpdgGain{} to \numSizeCtlQGain{}, and the QPDG \numSizeCtlYzGain{}, so its extra size buys little. Each of
the \numLcMissTotal{} units whose fault the QDG slice misses deletes an interaction the slice would follow, by
removing a control (\numLcMissCrm{}), moving an operand to another qubit (\numLcMissQsw{}), removing the preparation
of an entangling gate (\numLcMissEnt{}) or deleting a gate (\numLcMissDel{}). The QPDG contains
\numLcMissYzContained{} of them, presumably because its closure still links operations the faulty program no longer
connects.

State-based pruning lowers containment by \numHFourDiffAbs{} percentage points against the QDG slice (95\% CI
\numHFourCILow{} to \numHFourCIHigh{}, \numCcHFour{} with families as clusters), so H4, which allowed a loss of at most
five points, fails (exact McNemar, \numHFourOnlyLc{} against \numHFourOnlyQ{} discordant units, Holm
\numHFourPHolm{}). In \numCQFour{} units the QDG slice contains the fault and the state-based slice does not. A fault that breaks a correlation can
make the faulty operation look irrelevant to a single criterion qubit, and a joint criterion over all wrong qubits
recovers \numCQFourRecovered{} of these units. Where both slices contain the fault, the state-based slice
is smaller by \numHFiveSizeDiff{} operations on average (Wilcoxon, Holm \numHFivePHolm{}), so H5 holds for size, but
its backward-order score is worse by \numHFiveExamQHigher{} (\numHFiveExamP{}), so it fails for the rank.

Slices do not improve mutation-based fault localization. On the \numMbflAllN{} units whose fault every slice contains,
ranking within any slice reaches the fault after \numMbflAllExaminedMin{} to \numMbflAllExaminedMax{} operations on
average, as ranking over the whole circuit does, and since EXAM divides by the number of operations ranked, the whole
circuit scores lowest.

On the \numLinesUnits{} real Bugs4Q units whose faulty lines map to the program, the QDG line slices contain a faulty
line in every unit and keep \numLinesLcMean{} of \numLinesFullMean{} lines on average, so on these small programs slicing barely reduces
the reading. Def-use, which equals the classical PDG on these unitary programs, loses the faulty line in
\numLinesDuMissed{} of the units.

\paragraph*{Language models}
Asked to name the faulty operation, the Claude models do worse with every slice than with the whole circuit. Sonnet has the fault in its top three for
\numTTwoSonnetFull{} of the units with the whole circuit, \numTTwoSonnetLc{} with the QDG slice and \numTTwoSonnetQ{}
with the state-based slice, and Haiku for \numTTwoHaikuFull{}, \numTTwoHaikuLc{} and \numTTwoHaikuQ{}, while the Qwen
models change by less than a point with the QDG slice. In this exploratory analysis, a slice does not help a model localize a fault.

\emph{Answer to RQ3.} The QDG slice contains the fault in \numRqThreeContainLc{} of the units and misses only faults
that delete the interaction it would follow. State-based pruning loses \numHFourDiffAbs{} points of containment, and
no slice improves mutation-based fault localization or a language model's.

\subsection{RQ4: What does each rule cost in time as circuits grow?}

Computed from the circuit by the one-pass algorithm, the QDG slice takes \numOnePassMedianMs{}~ms per criterion in the
median over the \numOnePassCircuits{} circuits of the depth family, the width sweeps and corpus~L,
\numOnePassDeepMedianMs{}~ms at the largest depth and at most \numOnePassMaxMs{}~ms, and slicing every qubit of a
circuit takes at most \numOnePassAllMaxMs{}~ms.
State-based pruning, which simulates the circuit for every candidate removal, exceeds \numCostWallLimit{}~s for \numCostDepthEightyOverLimit{} of the \numCostDepthCircuits{} circuits at the second-largest depth and for all
\numCostDepthOneSixtyOverLimit{} at the largest, takes up to \numCostWidthQMax{}~s on the width sweeps at
\numCostWidthMaxQubits{} qubits, and completes on
\numCorpusLCompleted{} of the \numCorpusLCircuits{} circuits of corpus~L. Built as graphs, the QDG and the QPDG differ by two to three orders of magnitude. The QDG's graph takes
\numQdgBuildDepthFortyMs{}~ms at depth \numCostMeasuredDepthForty{} and \numQdgBuildDepthOneSixtyMs{}~ms at depth
\numCostMeasuredDepthOneSixty{} in the median, and the QPDG's \numCostBuildDepthForty{}~s and
\numCostBuildDepthOneSixty{}~s, because its closure links every pair of operations on a coupling component. An
implementation of the QPDG that avoided building the closure would be faster, but its slices would still be the larger
ones of RQ1.

\emph{Answer to RQ4.} The QDG slice takes milliseconds at every depth studied, while state-based pruning and the
construction of the QPDG grow to seconds and beyond the limit as circuits deepen.
