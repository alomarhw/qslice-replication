Program slicing helps developers understand code by showing which statements can affect a value of interest. For quantum programs, slicing has been proposed over a quantum program dependence graph (QPDG), but no definition of quantum dependence has been checked against what quantum programs compute. We lay foundations for quantum program slicing. We define quantum dependence semantically, introduce the quantum dependence graph (QDG), in which every gate reads and writes all its qubits, prove its slices sound and dependence-complete, and carry forward slices, conditioned slices and slice profiles over to qubits. We then evaluate dependence definitions against this ground truth on 784 circuit–qubit pairs and 5,688 units from seeded faults. The classical program dependence graph gives wrong slices for 26.0% of the pairs, because it misses phase kickback. The QPDG is sound but keeps 78.6% of a circuit, while QDG slices keep 70.4%, within 4.8 percentage points of the dependences themselves. State-based pruning, which simulates one input, shrinks slices by 23.2%, but its slices hold only for that input, drop the faulty operation in 769 units, and lower the fault-localization accuracy of three of four language models by up to 14.5 percentage points. A criterion over a set of qubits recovers 691 of these units. Our static slicer applies the QDG to Qiskit source, computes backward and forward slices and qubit profiles, and reports the lines and loop iterations a developer must read. We close with a roadmap of open problems.
