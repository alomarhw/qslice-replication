\section{Threats to Validity}\label{sec:threats}

\paragraph*{Construct validity}
We measure dependence by perturbing each operation at \numInfluenceInputs{} inputs. Finite sampling can
miss a dependence that shows only at other inputs, which would understate the dependences every rule
misses and overstate every rule's precision. It cannot make an unsound slice look sound, because
soundness is judged by the oracle's fidelity, not by the measured dependences, and the wire rule's
soundness for all inputs is proved. The other rules are judged at $\ket{0\dots0}$ and on \numRobDraws{}
draws per input regime, so they could be wrong at inputs we did not draw. The fidelity threshold of
$1 - 10^{-6}$ is far above the oracle's error of \numOracleErr{}. Whether the slice contains the fault site
is a proxy for whether it helps a developer, so we add mutation-based ranking and language models, but we
ran no study with developers. The language models saw one prompt at temperature~0.

\paragraph*{Internal validity}
Yu and Zhao's graph is described in prose, and we implemented our reading of it, with every interpretation
choice recorded before the runs. A different reading could change its precision, but not its soundness,
since its data edges alone already follow every qubit of a gate. The oracle shares no code with the
slicers and agrees with Qiskit's simulation. The baselines and variants added after the first runs enter
none of the five confirmatory tests. The benchmark circuits may be in the language models' training data,
which would favor the whole circuit and is the same for every slice.

\paragraph*{External validity}
Exact simulation limits corpus~A to \numMaxQubits{} qubits. Corpus~L extends the cost measurements to
20 qubits but not the soundness checks, and Theorem~\ref{thm:sound} covers any width. The circuits come
from benchmark suites rather than applications, and the real bugs are few and small. All programs are
Qiskit programs, and the dependence rules are stated on circuits, so they carry over to other languages
once their programs are lowered to circuits. A program whose loops depend on measurement outcomes, such as
a repeat-until-success loop in Microsoft's language Q\#~\cite{svore2018qsharp}, which repeats a subcircuit
until a measurement succeeds, has no single circuit, and a static slicer must over-approximate its
iterations. Seeded faults may differ from real ones. Nine of our ten operators come from three published
fault models.
