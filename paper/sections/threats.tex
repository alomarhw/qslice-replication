\section{Threats to Validity}\label{sec:threats}

\paragraph*{Construct validity}
We measure dependence by perturbing each operation at \numInfluenceInputs{} inputs. Finite sampling can
miss a dependence that shows only at other inputs, which would overstate every rule's recall and
understate its precision. Rerun with \numGcInputs{} inputs, ten of them entangled, and \numGcPerturbations{}
perturbations, the procedure adds \numGcDepsNew{} of \numGcDepsBefore{} dependences
(\numGcDepsNewPct{}), all inside QDG slices. No rule's mean recall moves by more than
\numGcRecallShiftQ{}, and the precision of the QDG rises only to \numGcPrecisionLc{}. It cannot make an unsound slice look sound, because
soundness is judged by the oracle's fidelity, not by the measured dependences, and the QDG's
soundness for all inputs is proved. The other rules are judged at $\ket{0\dots0}$ and on \numRobDraws{}
draws per input regime, so they could be wrong at inputs we did not draw. The fidelity threshold of
$1 - 10^{-6}$ is far above the oracle's error of \numOracleErr{}. Whether the slice contains the fault site
is a proxy for whether it helps a developer, so we add mutation-based ranking, but we ran no study with
developers. Pairs and units are qubits of the same circuits and mutants, and several circuits implement one
algorithm at different widths, so intervals that treat them as independent are too narrow. Resampling
whole families widens them but changes no conclusion.

\paragraph*{Internal validity}
Yu and Zhao's graph is described in prose, and every choice of our reading was recorded before the runs.
Since the two readings of its data dependence differ in soundness, claims about the QPDG hold for the
reading named. State-based
pruning compares quantities with $10^{-9}$, and on one Bugs4Q program rounding flips some of its decisions
between runs. On corpus~A, three fresh runs reproduce all \numQRepeatPairs{} slices.

\paragraph*{External validity}
Exact simulation limits corpus~A to \numMaxQubits{} qubits. Corpus~L extends the cost measurements to
20 qubits and the soundness checks to its \numCorpusLSoundCircuits{} circuits that the simulator can check,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:sound} covers any width. The circuits come
from benchmark suites rather than applications, and the real bugs are few and small. All programs are
Qiskit programs, and the dependence rules are stated on circuits, so they carry over to other languages
once their programs are lowered to circuits. A program whose loops depend on measurement outcomes, such as
a repeat-until-success loop in Microsoft's language Q\#~\cite{svore2018qsharp}, which repeats a subcircuit
until a measurement succeeds, has no single circuit, and a static slicer must over-approximate its
iterations. Seeded faults may differ from real ones. Nine of our ten operators come from three published
fault models.
