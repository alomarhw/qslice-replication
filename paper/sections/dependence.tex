\section{Quantum Dependence and Slicing}\label{sec:dependence}

This section defines what a slice of a circuit must preserve, the dependence of an operation on a qubit,
the rules we compare, the kinds of slices they yield, and the slice profile of a qubit. The definitions
are independent of any slicer, and Section~\ref{sec:results} measures each rule against them.

\subsection{Circuits and their semantics}
A circuit $P = o_0 \dots o_{L-1}$ is a sequence of operations over qubits $Q = \{0, \dots, n-1\}$ and
classical bits $C$. Each operation $o$ acts on a set of \emph{locations} $\mathit{loc}(o) \subseteq
Q \cup C$. A gate acts on its qubits, a measurement on its qubit and the bit it writes, a reset on its
qubit, and a classically conditioned gate on its qubits and the bits its condition reads. For an input
state $\rho$ of the qubits, with every bit initially $0$, $\llbracket P \rrbracket(\rho)$ denotes the
unconditional final state, the joint state of qubits and bits averaged over measurement
outcomes. For $W \subseteq Q$ and $\overline{W} = (Q \setminus W) \cup C$,
$\rho_W = \operatorname{Tr}_{\overline{W}}(\rho)$ is the reduced state of $W$, which determines every
measurement statistic of $W$. Every slicer returns a set of positions $S$, and the slice is judged by running the restriction
$P|_S$, which keeps the operations at the positions in $S$ in their original order.

\subsection{Criteria, slices and dependence}

\begin{definition}[Slicing criterion]\label{def:criterion}
A slicing criterion is a nonempty set $W \subseteq Q$ of qubits, whose state is taken at the end of the
circuit, before any terminal measurement reads it.
\end{definition}

Taking the state before readout keeps a slice correct when it is composed with later gates or read in
another basis. A criterion at an intermediate point is that of the prefix up to that point, and criteria
over classical bits are left to Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

Yu and Zhao keep the classical criterion $(s, V)$~\cite{yu2025qpdg}, but a qubit has no value of its
own apart from the qubits it is correlated with, so our criterion is a set of qubits whose \emph{joint}
state is preserved.

\begin{definition}[State preservation and soundness]\label{def:sound}
A set of positions $S$ \emph{preserves} $W$ at input $\rho$ if
$\operatorname{Tr}_{\overline{W}}\llbracket P|_S \rrbracket(\rho) =
\operatorname{Tr}_{\overline{W}}\llbracket P \rrbracket(\rho)$. $S$ is a \emph{sound} slice for $W$ if
it preserves $W$ at every input.
\end{definition}

Fault localization needs a second guarantee, that the slice contains every place where a fault could
change the outcome.

\begin{definition}[Quantum dependence]\label{def:dep}
Operation $o_k$ is a \emph{dependence} of $W$ in $P$ if there exist an input $\rho$ and a unitary $U$ on
the qubits of $o_k$ such that inserting $U$ immediately after $o_k$ changes
$\operatorname{Tr}_{\overline{W}}\llbracket P \rrbracket(\rho)$. For a measurement, inserting a classical
negation of the bit it writes also counts as such a change.
The set $\mathit{Dep}_W(P)$ collects the dependences of $W$. A set $S$ is \emph{dependence-complete} for $W$ if
$\mathit{Dep}_W(P) \subseteq S$.
\end{definition}

This is semantic dependence in the sense of Podgurski and Clarke~\cite{podgurski1990formal}, stated for
quantum states. Soundness and dependence-completeness are distinct. Deleting a single unitary operation
$o_k \notin \mathit{Dep}_W(P)$ leaves $\rho_W$ unchanged, as inserting $U = o_k^{\dagger}$ shows, but a sound
slice need not contain every dependence. The ground truth of Section~\ref{sec:design} approximates the
existential quantifiers of Definition~\ref{def:dep} by sampled inputs and perturbations, so the measured
$\mathit{Dep}_W(P)$ is a subset of the true one.

\subsection{Dependence rules}

A \emph{dependence rule} $R$ assigns each operation the locations it reads, $r_R(o)$, and the
locations it writes, $w_R(o)$. Operation $o_j$ depends on $o_i$ under $R$ if $i < j$ and some location
in $w_R(o_i) \cap r_R(o_j)$ is not written by any operation between them. The backward slice
$B_R(W)$ is the set of operations from which the last writer of some $q \in W$ is reachable. A rule can
account for three kinds of dependence. \emph{Data} dependence follows a qubit from the operation that
last changed it. \emph{Control} dependence follows a classical bit from the measurement that wrote it to
the operations its value conditions. \emph{Quantum} dependence lets a multi-qubit gate change every qubit
it acts on, including its controls, as phase kickback does. We compare three rules and two refinements of the most complete one.

The \emph{classical PDG}, written CPDG in subscripts, applies the classical program dependence
graph~\cite{ferrante1987pdg} to the circuit, with data and control dependence. Its data dependence reads
a gate as a classical assignment. A controlled gate reads its controls and targets and writes only its
targets, and any other gate reads and writes all its qubits. A measurement writes its bit, and a
conditioned gate reads the bits its condition tests. We also report \emph{def-use} (DU), the same data
dependence without classical bits, as the simplest transfer of classical slicing.

The \emph{quantum dependence graph} (QDG) adds quantum dependence to the classical PDG. Its nodes are
the operations, and every operation reads and writes all its locations,
$r_{\mathrm{QDG}}(o) = w_{\mathrm{QDG}}(o) = \mathit{loc}(o)$, so a controlled gate also writes its
controls. The QDG slice $\Bw(W)$ is backward reachability in the QDG from $W$. It is the backward light
cone of $W$, extended along classical bits from measurements to the gates they condition. LintQ orders
operations by this relation to find bug patterns~\cite{paltenghi2024lintq}, and Qiskit's
\texttt{LightCone} pass starts from it before it prunes~\cite{qiskit2025}. Theorem~\ref{thm:sound} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:complete} show that it is exactly what a slice must keep.

Fig.~\ref{fig:ansatz} applies the rules to one program that needs both control and quantum dependence.
An ansatz is the parameterized circuit of a variational algorithm such as the variational quantum
eigensolver (VQE), whose rotation angles a classical optimizer tunes. The ansatz of Fig.~\ref{fig:ansatz}a
has layers of rotations and a chain of CNOTs, and after them an auxiliary qubit is measured mid-circuit and
$q_0$ is flipped when the outcome is $1$, on lines~17 to~20.

The \emph{QPDG} is our reimplementation of Yu and Zhao's graph~\cite{yu2025qpdg} as a baseline. Its
dependences are given in prose, so we logged every choice of its primary reading before any run. Data and
control dependence are as in the QDG. The QPDG gives no rule for when two qubits are entangled, so we use
the flow-insensitive rule of entanglement analysis, under which qubits joined by a two-qubit gate anywhere in the circuit are entangled, and add an edge from every
operation to every later one on qubits of the same such component. A flow-sensitive variant counts two
qubits as entangled only from the first gate that couples them onward. Either way the QPDG is the QDG plus entanglement edges, so its slices contain
those of the QDG, and the hatched operations in Fig.~\ref{fig:ansatz}b are what the extra edges cost. Yu and Zhao do not say whether a gate writes its control, so we also run,
as an exploratory addition, the reading in which it does not. Table~\ref{tab:rules} lists the rules.

\emph{Commutation pruning} propagates the Pauli observables $X$, $Y$ and $Z$ of every criterion qubit
backward and removes from $\Bw(W)$ each operation that commutes with all of them, since such an operation
cannot change a measurement statistic of the criterion. Qiskit's \texttt{LightCone} pass implements
it~\cite{qiskit2025}.

\emph{State-based pruning} fixes one input, by default $\ket{0\dots0}$, and starts from $\Bw(W)$. It
removes an operation that does not act on the criterion if the mutual information between the criterion
and the operation's qubits is below $10^{-9}$ and the removal still preserves $W$ at that input.

\subsection{Properties}

\begin{theorem}[The QDG is sound]\label{thm:sound}
For every circuit $P$ and criterion $W$, $\Bw(W)$ is a sound slice for $W$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}[Proof sketch]
Process $P$ backward with a set of needed locations $N$, initially $W$. Operation $o_k$ joins the slice,
and $N$ grows by $\mathit{loc}(o_k)$, exactly when $\mathit{loc}(o_k) \cap N \neq \emptyset$, which
computes $\Bw(W)$. Every gate, reset, measurement and conditioned gate is a channel that acts only on
$\mathit{loc}(o)$. By locality of the partial trace, the no-signaling principle, an operation outside the
slice cannot change the reduced state of the needed locations, and an operation in the slice maps the
reduced state of the needed set before it to that after it. Induction over $P$ gives the claim. The
induction is mechanized in Isabelle/HOL for an abstract model of local operations, and that quantum
operations satisfy the model is argued on paper.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{proposition}[The QDG is dependence-complete]\label{prop:complete}
$\mathit{Dep}_W(P) \subseteq \Bw(W)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Let $o_k \notin \Bw(W)$, and let $P'$ insert $U$ after $o_k$. Then $U \notin \Bw(W)$ in $P'$, since
$o_k$ precedes it on the same locations, so $P'$ and $P$ have the same QDG slice and by
Theorem~\ref{thm:sound} the same reduced state of $W$. Negated outcomes are handled alike.
\end{IEEEproof}

On the ansatz of Fig.~\ref{fig:ansatz}, def-use ignores the classical bits, so its slice of $q_0$ keeps only \numAnsDuOps{} of the \numAnsOps{} operations, with fidelity
\numAnsDuFidelity{}, and loses the auxiliary's gate and measurement, which the classical PDG reaches
through the green control edge in Fig.~\ref{fig:ansatz}b.

\begin{proposition}[The classical PDG is unsound]\label{prop:du}
There are circuits for which neither $B_{\mathrm{CPDG}}(W)$ nor $B_{\mathrm{DU}}(W)$ preserves $W$, even
at $\ket{0\dots0}$.
\end{proposition}

The ansatz of Fig.~\ref{fig:ansatz} is a witness for both. On unitary circuits the two slices are equal.

Control dependence is not enough, however. A controlled operation acts coherently on a superposition of
its controls~\cite{svore2018qsharp}, so each \texttt{cx(qubit, qubit+1)} of the ansatz changes its
control as well as its target, and the state of $q_0$ comes to depend on the rotations of $q_1$ and, from
the second layer on, of $q_2$. The classical PDG treats a control as read-only, so its slice of $q_0$
keeps only the \numAnsCpdgOps{} dark- and mid-blue nodes of
Fig.~\ref{fig:ansatz}b, and misses \numAnsCpdgMissed{} dependences, with fidelity \numAnsCpdgFidelity{}.
Where the QDG has a blue quantum edge out of a CNOT, the classical PDG has a dotted edge that skips the
CNOT. The QDG slice keeps the \numAnsWireOps{} operations that are dependences of $q_0$, and no other.

\begin{proposition}[Entanglement is neither necessary nor sufficient for dependence]\label{prop:ent}
(i) An operation can be a dependence of $q$ although, at an input that witnesses the dependence, $q$ is
never entangled with its qubits.
(ii) An operation can act on a qubit entangled with $q$ and not be a dependence of $q$.
\end{proposition}

For (i), at input $\ket{0\dots0}$, $q_0$ in the program of Fig.~\ref{fig:bv} is in a pure state after
every operation, in the buggy and the fixed version, so it is never entangled. Yet the gates of lines~24
and~25 on the auxiliary and the CNOT of line~40 are dependences of $q_0$, since restoring $Z$ on line~25
changes its state. For (ii), in $\mathrm{H}(q_0)\,\mathrm{CX}(q_0, q_1)\,\mathrm{X}(q_1)$ the two qubits
are maximally entangled, but by no-signaling $\mathrm{X}(q_1)$ cannot change $\rho_{q_0}$. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:complete}, the entanglement edges of the QPDG add no dependence that the QDG misses,
and by (ii) they can add operations that are not dependences. In Fig.~\ref{fig:ansatz}b the QPDG adds the
\numAnsYzExtra{} hatched operations of $q_2$ in the second layer, which run after the last gate that
carries information from $q_2$ to $q_0$.

\begin{proposition}[The QDG distributes over criteria]\label{prop:joint}
$\Bw(W_1 \cup W_2) = \Bw(W_1) \cup \Bw(W_2)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
In the backward pass of Theorem~\ref{thm:sound}, the needed set for $W_1 \cup W_2$ is at every position
the union of those for $W_1$ and $W_2$.
\end{IEEEproof}

\paragraph*{What is not a dependence}
Entanglement does not decide dependence, by Proposition~\ref{prop:ent}, and the other proposed sources
add no edges. A CNOT that copies a basis state, the case no-cloning concerns, is a two-qubit gate whose
edges data dependence already has. Timing decides when an operation runs on hardware, not what a state
depends on. Superposition changes amplitudes, not which qubits an operation
acts on~\cite{nielsen2010quantum}, so it cannot enlarge a dependence graph. Only state-based pruning
uses amplitudes, at a cost exponential in the circuit's width.

\subsection{Kinds of slices}
Classical slicing knows other kinds of slices besides the backward one~\cite{tip1994a, silva2012vocabulary},
and each carries over to quantum programs differently.

\paragraph*{Forward slices}
The forward slice $\Fw(o_k)$ of an operation is the set of operations reachable from $o_k$ in the QDG. It
tells a developer who changes a gate which later operations and qubits the change can reach. We say that
$o_k$ reaches $q$ if $\Fw(o_k)$ contains the last operation on $q$.

\begin{proposition}[Forward and backward slices are dual]\label{prop:dual}
For every operation $o_k$ and qubit $q$, $o_k$ reaches $q$ if and only if $o_k \in \Bw(\{q\})$. Hence
$o_k$ reaches every qubit of which it is a dependence.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Both sides say that the QDG has a path from $o_k$ to the last operation on $q$. The second claim follows
from Proposition~\ref{prop:complete}.
\end{IEEEproof}

\paragraph*{Undirected slices}
Following QDG edges in both directions from the operations on $q$ gives a sound slice, since it contains
$\Bw(\{q\})$, but Section~\ref{sec:results} shows that it keeps almost the whole circuit, so it is a
pitfall rather than a decomposition of the program by qubit.

\paragraph*{Joint criteria}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:joint}, a tool needs to compute only single-qubit QDG slices. The criterion
still matters for pruning. State-based pruning for $\{q\}$ preserves $\rho_q$ but not the correlations
of $q$ with other qubits, so it can delete an operation that changes $\rho_W$ for a larger $W$.

\paragraph*{Static and conditioned slices}
State-based pruning preserves the criterion only at its one input, so it computes a conditioned
slice~\cite{canfora1998conditioned}, here a dynamic slice~\cite{korel1988dynamic} obtained as in
observation-based slicing~\cite{binkley2014orbs}. The restriction matters when the slice is reused in a
larger circuit or its angles are bound anew, the regimes Section~\ref{sec:results} tests.

\paragraph*{Granularity}
A slice can be reported as operations, loop iterations, or source lines. Operations are the unit of the
definitions and lines the unit of the developer. A line is in the slice if one of its operations is, so a
line-level slice can keep code that no operation-level slice needs. In the second layer of the ansatz of
Fig.~\ref{fig:ansatz}, the rotations of $q_2$ are not dependences of $q_0$, yet lines 12 and 13 are in
the slice because their first-layer iterations are, so \tool{} also reports the iterations of each line.
A slice of operations is executable, and a slice of lines marks the code to read.

\subsection{Qubit slice profiles}\label{sec:profiles}
We define the analogue for a qubit $q$ of the variable slice profile of srcSlice~\cite{alomari2014srcslice}. Its uses are the lines of the operations on $q$, each of which both uses and
defines $q$ under the QDG. Its names are the source expressions that denote $q$ in gate calls, and its
functions are the call sites of the functions inside which $q$ is acted on. Its dependents are the other
qubits that the first operation on $q$ reaches, transitively.
Its bits are those measured from $q$, with the lines conditioned on them, and its backward and forward
entries are the lines of the backward slice of $q$ and of the forward slice of its first operation. In
the program of Fig.~\ref{fig:bv}, \tool{} computes the profile of
$q_0$ as $\mathsf{uses}(q_0) = \numBvProfDefsSet{}$, $\mathsf{functions}(q_0) = \emptyset$,
$\mathsf{names}(q_0) = \{{\texttt{i} \mapsto \numBvProfNameISet{}}, {\texttt{q} \mapsto \numBvProfNameQSet{}}\}$,
$\mathsf{dependents}(q_0) = \numBvProfDependentsSet{}$, $\mathsf{bits}(q_0) = \numBvProfBitsSet{}$ with no
conditioned lines, $\mathsf{backward}(q_0) = \numBvProfBackwardSet{}$ and $\mathsf{forward}(q_0) =
\numBvProfForwardSet{}$, where every set except the qubits and bits holds line numbers. A profile gathers
in one view what a developer first asks about a qubit, and it costs one backward and one forward pass.
