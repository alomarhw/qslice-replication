\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph*{Program slicing}
Weiser introduced slicing~\cite{weiser1984program} and showed that programmers use slices when they
debug~\cite{weiser1982programmers}. Dependence graphs reduced it to
reachability~\cite{ottenstein1984pdg, ferrante1987pdg, horwitz1990interprocedural}, and dynamic slicing
restricts a slice to one execution~\cite{korel1988dynamic, agrawal1990dynamic}, as state-based pruning does
to one input. Frameworks such as WALA provide dependence-graph slicers for Java, JavaScript
and Python~\cite{wala}, but on a Qiskit program they see each gate as a call on one circuit object and cannot
tell which qubits an operation depends on. srcSlice computes lightweight forward slices and slice profiles of every variable from srcML without
building a dependence graph~\cite{alomari2014srcslice}. \tool{} extends it to quantum programs and takes a slice's
classical lines from it, but srcSlice alone has no notion of qubits. Gallagher names quantum programs as a direction for semantically
defined slicing~\cite{gallagher2025program}, and we define the slice by the reduced state of the criterion. Probabilistic slicing preserves the distribution of the criterion
variables~\cite{hur2014slicing, amtoft2020theory, olmedo2025slicing}. Our criterion is stronger, since a
distribution in one basis does not see phase or the kickback of Section~\ref{sec:example}.

\paragraph*{Dependence in quantum programs}
To our knowledge, Yu and Zhao's QPDG~\cite{yu2025qpdg} is the only prior proposal of dependence-based
slicing for quantum programs. Its dependences are defined in prose and were not implemented or evaluated. Its no-cloning example, \texttt{x(0)} followed by \texttt{cx(0,1)}, is described as entangling,
although it produces the product state $\ket{11}$, which is case~(i) of Proposition~\ref{prop:ent}. In their
graph for Listing~1, the measurement of $q_1$ misses \texttt{x(1)} and the second CNOT, which make it
return 1 with certainty, while the implied slice does so with probability \numYzFigSliceProb{}. We implement it as a documented baseline. The quantum code property graph of Kaul et al.~\cite{kaul2023uniform} treats the control of a CNOT as
unchanged, the reading that phase kickback breaks, as Proposition~\ref{prop:du} shows. LintQ orders
two operations exactly when they share a qubit~\cite{paltenghi2024lintq}, the relation of the QDG, to find
bug patterns without aiming at full soundness, and we show that this relation gives sound and
dependence-complete slices. Behler et al.\ extend srcML to OpenQASM~\cite{behler2024supporting}. Their QStatic tool orders the
operations of each qubit in hand-marked-up OpenQASM and uses that order to find patterns and
refactorings, and they name qubit slicing with srcML's slicer as future work~\cite{behler2025static}.
QStatic has no dependence rule and does not read Qiskit, and \tool{} realizes that future work with a
dependence rule that is proved sound.

\paragraph*{Entanglement analysis}
Static analyses approximate which qubits may be entangled with type
systems~\cite{perdrix2007types, prost2009reasoning, yuan2022twist}, abstract
interpretation~\cite{perdrix2008entanglement, yu2021abstract}, the stabilizer
formalism~\cite{honda2015stabilizer} and dataflow rules~\cite{xia2025entanglement}. ScaffCC assumes that
interacting qubits may be entangled~\cite{javadiabhari2015scaffcc, zhao2023qchecker}, the entanglement
rule of our QPDG baseline. These analyses are not slicers, and Proposition~\ref{prop:ent} shows that
whether qubits are correlated does not decide dependence.

\paragraph*{Light cones and compilers}
Heim et al.\ note that quantum dependences should account for commutation~\cite{heim2020quantum}, and Qiskit's \texttt{LightCone} pass keeps the gates that can reach an
observable and drops those that commute with it~\cite{qiskit2025}. Compilers build gate-dependence graphs~\cite{murali2019triq, amy2020staq}, QAOA analyses use light-cone
arguments~\cite{farhi2014qaoa}, and quantum separation logics reason locally about the qubits a program
touches~\cite{zhou2021qsl, le2022qsl}. None defines what a slice must keep or proves it
sound, and Theorem~\ref{thm:sound} states the light-cone fact as a property of slices.

\paragraph*{Debugging quantum programs}
A roadmap of quantum debugging lists slicing as an opportunity and finds almost no use of dependence
analysis~\cite{ramalho2025road}. The slicing of Metwalli and Van
Meter~\cite{metwalli2022debugging, metwalli2024testing} splits a circuit at breakpoints the developer places,
and Qiskit's slicing utilities partition it by barriers, depth or gate type~\cite{qiskitaddonutils}. Neither
has a criterion, so the developer still narrows the fault by hand. Metwalli and Van Meter's phase tests detect a wrong phase
but need hand-designed test vectors to locate the faulty qubit. Bugs4Q~\cite{zhao2023bugs4q} catalogs real faults,
QChecker~\cite{zhao2023qchecker} and Q-PAC~\cite{nayak2023qpac} detect known patterns, and mutation tools and fault models~\cite{mendiluze2021muskit, fortunato2022qmutpy, garcia2022testing} and
mutation-based fault localization~\cite{ishimoto2025mbfl} supply our faults and ranking. Slicing restricts
where these tools look, although RQ3 shows that a restriction alone does not improve their normalized ranking.
