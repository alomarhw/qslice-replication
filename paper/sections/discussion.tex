\section{Implications for Comprehension Tools}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph*{What a quantum slice should look like}
A slicer for a developer who asks which lines determine a qubit should show the QDG slice, which is sound for every input, misses no dependence and costs milliseconds. Reading a
gate as a classical assignment is the pitfall to avoid, because its slices can look right and hide the
fault, and adding control dependence does not remove it. Entanglement edges buy no soundness that the QDG lacks
and add code that cannot change the criterion's state, as Proposition~\ref{prop:ent} shows. The slice
should be shown as the lines, loop iterations and classical lines a developer reads, with the
over-approximations the analysis made, and a slicer should let the developer name a set of qubits, whose
joint QDG slice needs no extra analysis by Proposition~\ref{prop:joint}. No dependence slice of a faulty
program can show an interaction the program omits, and every fault the QDG slice misses removes one, so
the slicer should say that a fault may be a missing gate or control. A smaller view can come from
commutation pruning with symbolic angles, which stays correct when the angles change. State-based pruning
should be offered, if at all, as a view of one input, labeled as such, and a slice should be checked by the
criterion's state, not by its histogram.

\paragraph*{What is open}
The first open problem is precision for every input, which needs
static analyses that reason about states, such as commutation valid for all angles or abstract
interpretation of stabilizer states. The second is slices for classes of inputs, as conditioned and
quasi-static slicing~\cite{canfora1998conditioned, venkatesh1991semantic} suggest. The third is hybrid
programs, where a slice across the loop of a variational algorithm needs interprocedural dependence through
measured results~\cite{horwitz1990interprocedural}.
The fourth is chopping~\cite{jackson1994chopping},
dicing~\cite{lyle1987dicing}, amorphous slices~\cite{harman1997amorphous} and criteria over classical
bits or observables, which need definitions against the semantics of Section~\ref{sec:dependence}. The fifth is other languages and compiled code, for which srcML's markup for
OpenQASM~\cite{behler2024supporting, behler2025static} gives a front end. The sixth is evidence with
developers. Our localization results use seeded faults and the small programs of Bugs4Q, and a study of
developers comprehending and debugging quantum programs with slices is the next step, as it was for
classical slicing~\cite{weiser1982programmers}.
