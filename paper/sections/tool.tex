\section{A Static Slicer for Qiskit Programs}\label{sec:tool}

Developers write Qiskit programs, not circuits, and a slice helps them only as lines of their program. We
therefore built a tool that slices a Qiskit program from its source, without running it, with the QDG
of Section~\ref{sec:dependence}. Fig.~\ref{fig:overview} shows its steps on the program of
Section~\ref{sec:example}.

\paragraph*{Parsing}
The tool parses the program with srcML~\cite{collard2011lightweight, collard2013srcml}, which turns source
code into XML that keeps every character of the source and marks its syntax and the position of each
element. A gate call such as \texttt{circuit.cx(0,1)} becomes a \texttt{call} element whose name gives the
gate and whose arguments give its qubits, as stage~2 of Fig.~\ref{fig:overview} shows.

\paragraph*{Static interpretation}
The tool walks the srcML tree and turns it into an operation trace, as the execution-order analysis of
Behler et al.\ does for OpenQASM, the assembly-like language of quantum circuits~\cite{behler2024supporting}.
It evaluates the program's expressions over an abstract domain of known values, registers, bits and
circuits. It unrolls loops and records the iteration of each operation, inlines functions, and follows
\texttt{if\_test} and \texttt{c\_if}, Qiskit's two constructs for gates conditioned on measured bits, as
Fig.~\ref{fig:loops} shows for the ansatz. Each value carries the lines that computed it, so the tool knows
the classical lines, the statements that compute an operation's qubits, angles and conditions. The QDG
 does not need angle values, so angles drawn at run time stay symbolic. Where a value cannot be
resolved statically, the tool over-approximates. An unknown qubit index makes the operation act on every
qubit it may denote, and an unknown condition runs every branch. A loop over an unknown range repeats its
body once more than the number of qubits the body touches, which covers every chain of dependences through
the loop. Because the QDG never removes a qubit from the needed set, these over-approximations can
only enlarge a slice, and the tool reports each of them. When a circuit is passed to code the tool does not
model, such as a rewrite of its directed acyclic graph (DAG) representation, the tool reports it as not
traced.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{QDG slice of a static operation trace}\label{alg:slice}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require trace $o_0 \dots o_{L-1}$, criterion $W$, a set of qubits
\State $N \gets W$; \ $S \gets \emptyset$ \Comment{needed locations, slice}
\For{$k \gets L-1$ \textbf{down to} $0$}
  \If{$o_k$ is a barrier or a terminal measurement} \textbf{continue} \EndIf
  \State $Q_k \gets$ every qubit $o_k$ may act on; $C_k \gets$ the bits $o_k$ measures into
  \If{$(Q_k \cup C_k) \cap N \neq \emptyset$}
    \State $S \gets S \cup \{k\}$
    \If{$o_k$ certainly writes a bit $b \in C_k$} $N \gets N \setminus \{b\}$ \EndIf
    \State $N \gets N \cup Q_k \cup \mathit{cond}(o_k)$ \Comment{reads all its qubits}
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $S$, the lines and iterations of $S$, and their classical lines
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph*{Slicing}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:slice} is the backward pass of Theorem~\ref{thm:sound} on the trace. It skips barriers,
which do not change the state, and terminal measurements, after which a qubit is not used. Because of
over-approximation, an operation may act on several candidate qubits. It adds all of them to $N$, and it
removes a bit from $N$ only when it certainly writes that bit. $\mathit{cond}(o_k)$ is the set of bits that
the condition of $o_k$ reads. The pass returns the operations of the slice, their lines and iterations, and
the classical lines they depend on. Its cost is linear in the length of the trace for a fixed criterion.

\paragraph*{Validation}
On \numToolDevPrograms{} programs that we wrote and can run, of up to \numToolDevMaxOps{} operations,
including Shor's order finding, Grover search, phase estimation, an adder, the Quantum Approximate
Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) and a repetition code, the static trace equals the circuit Qiskit builds. The
slice of every qubit equals the circuit-level QDG slice that the study uses on \numToolDevSliceEqual{} of
them. The remaining program nests conditional blocks, which the circuit-level slicer reads only by their
outer condition. On \numToolNestedDraws{} random product inputs, the state simulator of
Section~\ref{sec:design} finds the circuit-level slice wrong \numToolNestedStudyWrong{} times and the
tool's slice never. On the Bugs4Q programs of our study, read without being run, the trace equals the
prepared circuit for \numToolTOneExact{} of \numToolTOneBugs{} bugs. The remaining bug rewrites its circuit
as a DAG, which the tool reports. The tool's line slices equal those of the study on all
\numToolTTwoUnits{} units whose lines the study maps, which include the \numLinesUnits{} units of
Section~\ref{sec:results}, so the line-level results there are those of the tool. On all
\numToolBugsQiskit{} Qiskit programs of Bugs4Q, every program parses, \numToolBugsCircuit{} yield a
circuit, \numToolBugsResolved{} of them without any over-approximation, and none crashes the tool. The
others build no circuit or are fragments. On the \numToolTextbookQiskit{} Qiskit notebooks of the Qiskit
textbook~\cite{qiskittextbook}, \numToolTextbookCircuit{} yield a circuit, and on the
\numToolLintqQiskit{} Qiskit programs that the authors of the LintQ static analyzer collected from
GitHub~\cite{paltenghi2024lintq},
\numToolLintqCircuit{} yield a circuit, none crashes the tool, and \numToolLintqTimeouts{} exceeds
the 60~s limit, because its nested loops of unknown length multiply the copies of their bodies. The tool takes \numToolBugsMedianMs{}~ms
per Bugs4Q program and \numToolTextbookMedianMs{}~ms per textbook notebook in the median, srcML included.
