\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A slice of a quantum program is correct only if it follows data, control and quantum dependence. We
defined quantum dependence by the semantics of quantum programs and measured dependence definitions
against it. The classical PDG gives wrong slices for \numCpdgUnsoundPct{} of the pairs, because a
two-qubit gate can change its control. Following entanglement is sound but imprecise, because
entanglement is not sufficient for dependence, and it is not necessary either. Our quantum dependence graph
(QDG) is sound for every input, dependence-complete and cheap, and state-based pruning trades that guarantee for slices that
hold for one input and can remove the fault. Our static slicer shows that the QDG works on Qiskit
source, where it reports the lines and iterations a developer must read to understand a qubit's state,
and that forward slices and qubit profiles follow from the same graph. Whether such slices improve
comprehension needs a study with developers, the last item of the roadmap of
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}. Quantum slicers should start from
the QDG, take sets of qubits as criteria, and report precision beyond it only where it is safe. The
replication package at \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/qslice-replication-5B21} regenerates every number in this paper.
