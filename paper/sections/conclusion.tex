\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A slice of a quantum program is correct only if it follows data, control and quantum dependence. We
defined quantum dependence by the semantics of quantum programs and measured dependence definitions
against it. The classical PDG gives wrong slices for \numCpdgUnsoundPct{} of the pairs, because it reads the
control of a gate as unchanged. Adding entanglement edges keeps a slice sound but makes it imprecise,
because entanglement is not sufficient for dependence, and it is not necessary either. Our quantum dependence graph
(QDG) is sound for every input, dependence-complete and cheap, and state-based pruning trades that guarantee for slices that
hold for one input and can remove the fault. Our static slicer shows that the QDG works on Qiskit
source, where it reports the lines and iterations a developer must read to understand a qubit's state,
and that forward slices and qubit profiles follow from the same graph. Whether such slices improve
comprehension needs a study with developers, the last item of the roadmap of
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\section*{Data Availability}
The replication package at \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/qslice-replication-5B21} contains the protocol and its
deviations, the Isabelle/HOL theory of Theorem~\ref{thm:sound}, the static slicer, the benchmark circuits, the logs of every run with the commit it used, and
the scripts that regenerate every number, table and figure in this paper. Every random draw has a fixed
seed. The runs used Python~3.12.2 and Qiskit~2.3.0 on an Intel Xeon W-2140B workstation, with every
package version pinned in a lock file, and the package will be archived with a DOI on acceptance.
