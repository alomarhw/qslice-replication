\begin{abstract}
Program slicing helps developers understand code by showing which statements can affect a value of
interest. For quantum programs, slicing has been proposed over a quantum program dependence graph (QPDG),
but no definition of quantum dependence has been checked against what quantum programs compute. We define
quantum dependence semantically, introduce the quantum dependence graph (QDG), in which every gate reads
and writes all its qubits, and prove its slices sound and dependence-complete. We then evaluate
dependence definitions against this ground truth on \numNPairs{} circuit--qubit pairs and
\numRqThreeUnits{} units from seeded faults. The classical program dependence graph gives wrong slices
for \numCpdgUnsoundPct{} of the pairs, because it misses phase kickback. The QPDG is sound but keeps
\numRqOneYzSize{} of a circuit, while QDG slices keep \numRqOneLcSize{}, within \numLcExcess{}
percentage points of the dependences themselves. State-based pruning, which simulates one input, shrinks
slices by \numHTwoRelative{}, but its slices hold only for that input, drop the faulty operation in
\numCQFour{} units, and lower the fault-localization accuracy of three of four language models by up to
\numTTwoMaxDrop{} percentage points. A criterion over a set of qubits recovers \numCQFourRecovered{} of
these units. Our static slicer applies the QDG to Qiskit source and reports the lines and loop iterations
a developer must read.
\end{abstract}
