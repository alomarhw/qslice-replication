\begin{abstract}
A developer who sees a qubit end in the wrong state needs to know which lines of the program determine it,
the question program slicing answers for classical code. Slicing has been proposed for quantum programs,
but its dependence rules have never been checked against what a quantum program computes, and we find
that they can give slices that look right while hiding the fault. We ask what a quantum slice should look
like to support comprehension. A slice must preserve the joint state of the criterion qubits for every
input, and an operation is a dependence when changing it can change that state. Under these definitions
we prove that the quantum dependence graph (QDG), in which every gate reads and writes all of its qubits,
gives sound and dependence-complete slices, and we measure every alternative against it. On
\numNPairs{} circuit--qubit pairs, the classical program dependence graph, which reads the control of a
gate as unchanged, gives wrong slices for \numCpdgUnsoundPct{} of them, and \numFdCpdgZMatchPct{} of these
reproduce the measured outcomes, so a histogram check would accept them. Entanglement edges, as in the
quantum program dependence graph, keep \numRqOneYzSize{} of a circuit on average, against
\numRqOneLcSize{} for the QDG. The QDG keeps only \numLcExcess{} percentage points more than the dependences
themselves.
Pruning by simulating one input shrinks slices by a further \numHTwoRelative{}, but its slices hold only
for that input and lose a fault that the QDG slice keeps in \numCQFour{} of \numRqThreeUnits{}
seeded-fault units. On the programs of LintQ's corpus a qubit's slice holds \numScaleReadMedianPct{} of the
code lines in the median, while on the small Bugs4Q programs it holds almost all of them, and slices do not
improve mutation-based fault ranking. Our static slicer, \tool{}, extends srcML and srcSlice with quantum roles, execution order and the
QDG, reads Qiskit programs and OpenQASM~2 circuits without running them, and shows the slice on the developer's own
source as lines, loop iterations and qubit profiles. It reads a circuit of \numScaleQbMaxOps{} operations in
\numScaleQbReadS{}~s and slices all its qubits in \numScaleQbAllS{}~s, and its profiles expose \numLqQubitExposed{} of the \numLqQubitTp{} qubit bugs
that LintQ's authors confirmed on their corpus. We close with what comprehension tools should show.
\end{abstract}
