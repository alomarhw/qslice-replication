\begin{abstract}
Program slicing helps developers understand code by showing which statements can affect a value of
interest. For quantum programs, slicing has been proposed over a quantum program dependence graph (QPDG),
but no definition of quantum dependence has been checked against what quantum programs compute. We define
quantum dependence and slice soundness semantically, over the joint state of a set of qubits, and prove
that the quantum dependence graph (QDG), in which every gate reads and writes all its qubits, gives sound
and dependence-complete slices. The QDG is the known backward light cone, and its soundness follows from
the locality of quantum operations; what is new is the semantics it is measured against.
We also carry forward slices, conditioned slices and slice profiles over to qubits. We evaluate dependence
definitions against these semantics on \numNPairs{} circuit--qubit pairs and \numRqThreeUnits{} units from
seeded faults. The classical program dependence graph, which reads the control of a gate as unchanged,
gives wrong slices for \numCpdgUnsoundPct{} of the pairs. Our reimplementation of the QPDG is sound by construction but
keeps \numRqOneYzSize{} of a circuit, while QDG slices keep \numRqOneLcSize{}, within \numLcExcess{}
percentage points of the dependences themselves. State-based pruning, which simulates one input, shrinks
slices by \numHTwoRelative{}, but its slices hold only for that input and drop the faulty operation in
\numCQFour{} units, of which a criterion over all wrong qubits recovers \numCQFourRecovered{}. Our static
slicer applies the QDG to Qiskit source and computes backward and forward slices and qubit profiles. We
close with a roadmap of open problems.
\end{abstract}
