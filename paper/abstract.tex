\begin{abstract}
Program slicing helps developers understand code by showing which statements can affect a value of
interest, the question they ask when they debug. For quantum programs, slicing has been proposed by
reusing the classical slicing criterion and graph reachability over a quantum program dependence graph,
but no definition of quantum dependence has been checked against the semantics of quantum programs. We
define it semantically, as the set of operations where a change can alter a qubit's state for some input,
and use this ground truth to evaluate dependence definitions on \numNPairs{} circuit--qubit pairs from
three benchmark suites, \numFamCircuits{} circuits with known structure, and \numRqThreeUnits{}
fault--qubit units from \numMutantsKilled{} seeded faults. Even the full classical program dependence
graph, with data and control dependence, gives wrong slices for \numCpdgUnsoundPct{} of the pairs,
because it misses the effect of a gate on its control qubit, called phase kickback. Following
entanglement, as the proposed quantum program dependence graph (QPDG) does, is sound but keeps
\numRqOneYzSize{} of the circuit in our reimplementation, and \numYzfSize{} when entanglement is tracked
along the program. Our quantum dependence graph (QDG), in which every gate reads and writes all its qubits,
keeps \numRqOneLcSize{}, within \numLcExcess{} percentage points of the smallest slice that contains
every dependence. It misses no dependence and stays correct on every tested input, and its soundness
proof is mechanized for an abstract model. Commutation pruning shrinks these slices further, but stays
correct when rotation angles change only if it treats them as symbols. State-based pruning shrinks them
by \numHTwoRelative{}, but only for the input it was computed on. It removes the faulty operation in
\numCQFour{} units and lowers the fault-localization accuracy of three of four language models by up to
\numTTwoMaxDrop{} percentage points. A joint criterion over all wrong qubits recovers
\numCQFourRecovered{} of these units. Our static slicer applies the QDG to Qiskit source and
reports the lines and loop iterations a developer must read. Quantum dependence is not entanglement, and
the results give tool builders a sound, input-independent dependence rule and a slicing criterion that
fits quantum state.
\end{abstract}
